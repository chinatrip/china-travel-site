# Healthcare and Medical Preparation for China

For a medical emergency in China, call **120** for an ambulance. Prepare insurance, medicine arrangements and a local care plan before travelling. This guide provides general preparation information, not an individual diagnosis or treatment plan.

## How do I get care and pay for it?

The [UK government China health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china/health) confirms 120 and warns that healthcare is not free. Its [medical-treatment guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-treatment-in-china/medical-treatment-in-china) describes possible deposits and the need to arrange payment. Ask the hospital and insurer about direct billing, accepted payment methods and documents; do not assume your policy removes all upfront costs.

For planned care, confirm a facility's current services and language assistance directly. Take your identity document, medication/allergy list and insurer contact details. Keep itemised bills and records for a claim. This guide does not promise nationwide consultation prices, waiting times or deposit amounts.

## What should I prepare before travelling?

Discuss your itinerary and health needs with a clinician well before departure, including routine/travel vaccines and any high-altitude plans. Check insurance for your actual activities, treatment, evacuation and repatriation. Ask the insurer about exclusions, altitude limits and authorization procedures. Source: [government health advice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/china/health).

For prescription medicines, ask your clinician and the Chinese embassy whether the specific ingredient and quantity can enter China. Rules and availability can differ from home; a prescription is not blanket import permission. Carry the prescription and labelled packaging as preparation, and obtain any required approval before travel. Do not rely on a universal “seven-day supply” rule. Avoid untreated drinking water; the same government guide recommends bottled water.

## What should I do after an animal bite or scratch?

Immediately wash the wound thoroughly with soap and water and seek prompt medical assessment for possible rabies exposure. Do not wait for symptoms. A clinician decides whether post-exposure prophylaxis is needed; vaccine and rabies immune globulin requirements depend on previous vaccination and clinical circumstances. Source: [CDC rabies post-exposure guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/rabies/hcp/clinical-care/post-exposure-prophylaxis.html).

## Which altitude symptoms need urgent help?

Gradual ascent and time to acclimatize reduce risk. Do not ascend to sleep higher while experiencing altitude symptoms. Worsening symptoms despite rest require descent and medical help. **Breathlessness at rest, confusion or difficulty walking are urgent warning signs**, not routine symptoms to ignore. Seek emergency assistance and arrange safe descent; do not rely on small oxygen cans to manage serious illness. Discuss preventive medicine with a clinician rather than choosing a dose from a travel guide. Source: [CDC Yellow Book altitude guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html).

## Is Rhodiola a proven way to prevent altitude illness?

No reliable evidence establishes Rhodiola (红景天) as effective for a health purpose. NIH reports possible side effects, a reported interaction with **losartan**, and uncertain safety in pregnancy or breastfeeding. It should not replace acclimatization or medical care. Source: [NIH NCCIH Rhodiola summary](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/rhodiola).

[www.gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-treatment-in-china/medical-treatment-in-china)

## Does a rabies vaccination before travel remove the need for care after a bite?

No. Seek prompt medical attention after a possible exposure even if you completed a recognised vaccination series. CDC describes a shorter post-exposure regimen for previously vaccinated people that does not include rabies immune globulin. Discuss vaccination before travel if animal exposure is likely, including travel in Sichuan.

[www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/asia/china.html) · [www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-associated-infections-diseases/rabies.html)

## What can my prescriber put in a medication letter for travel?

Ask your prescriber to list each medicine’s generic name, dose and reason for use. Check China’s rules for the individual medicine separately.

[www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travelers-with-additional-considerations/traveling-with-prohibited-or-restricted-medications.html)

## When can mountain sickness start, and when should I avoid alcohol?

CDC says acute mountain sickness usually starts two to twelve hours after reaching a higher altitude, often during the first night. It advises avoiding alcohol for the first 48 hours at altitude.

[www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/high-altitude-travel-and-altitude-illness.html)
