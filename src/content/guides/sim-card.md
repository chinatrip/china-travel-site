# SIM Card and eSIM in China for Foreign Visitors

Foreign visitors have three connectivity options in mainland China, each with different setup, cost, and Great Firewall implications.

## Should I get an eSIM or a SIM card for China?

| Option | Best for | GFW bypass | Setup time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foreign travel eSIM** (Airalo, Holafly, Nomad eSIM, Ubigi) | Trips ≤ 30 days; visitors who don't need a +86 phone number and want zero VPN hassle | ✅ Usually — Airalo, Holafly and Nomad state their data routes outside mainland China, so Google / YouTube / Gmail / WhatsApp work without a separate VPN (checked October 2026); Ubigi's own materials are contradictory (see below) | ~2 minutes (QR install) |
| **Local physical SIM** | Longer stays; anyone who wants a +86 number for SMS codes from local lifestyle apps | ❌ No — Chinese-network traffic; needs a separate VPN for blocked sites | In person at a counter (passport + face scan) |
| **International roaming** | Short stays where cost matters less than convenience | Usually yes, if your carrier routes roaming data back through your home network — confirm with your carrier | None |

On roaming, a China SIM guide notes that "if your roaming uses home-country routing, it usually works; if your traffic is routed through local Chinese networks, it may still be blocked by the firewall" — and that roaming is usually expensive (chinaguidelines.com, checked 2026-10-08).

For most short trips a Chinese phone number matters less than you might expect. The same guide says core travel needs — "payments, ride-hailing, public transportation, hotel booking, and high-speed rail tickets usually do not require a Chinese phone number": Alipay, WeChat Pay and DiDi accept international numbers, and 12306 / Trip.com can use email registration. Where a +86 number still helps is some local lifestyle apps (Meituan, Dianping, Amap and similar), where foreign numbers are "supported inconsistently". See [our full guide to getting a Chinese phone number](/guides/chinese-phone-number/) for which services actually require one and how to obtain a +86 number.

## Which eSIM should I buy for China, and what does it cost?

> **Note:** Three of the four major foreign travel eSIMs marketed for China state in their own materials that data is routed outside mainland China, so blocked sites work with **no separate VPN** (checked 2026-10-08). Airalo's help centre: "a VPN is NOT required"; "your traffic is routed through secure gateways outside mainland China". Nomad: "no external VPN needed". Holafly's China page: you can use WhatsApp, Google and Instagram "without installing an additional VPN", and it also says a VPN is included in the plan. **Ubigi is unclear:** its China plan page no longer says anything about VPNs or the firewall, and its own China blog post (July 2026) says both that Ubigi routes data "through networks outside mainland China" so you "could access" Google and WhatsApp without a VPN, and, further down, that "an eSIM … does not bypass the Great Firewall" and "you will need a VPN". Ask Ubigi before relying on it. These are the providers' own statements, not an independent routing audit, and this is high-volatility — confirm the provider's current China page before buying. The bypass applies only to eSIM data — Chinese hotel/airport Wi-Fi is still behind the Great Firewall.

Prices are close, and what separates the four is plan shape and network rather than price. For a 7-day trip the unlimited plans run **$24–$27.50** (Ubigi $24, Nomad $25, Airalo $27, Holafly $27.50). Nomad's China page describes a daily high-speed allowance followed by 512 kbps for its per-day plans; check the plan details for its unlimited terms. **How to activate.** Install the eSIM by QR before departure. The plan normally starts when the eSIM first connects to a network in mainland China — Holafly, Nomad and Ubigi each say so — and you may need to turn on data roaming for the eSIM line. Nomad plans must be activated within 60 days of purchase or they start automatically; unactivated Ubigi plans expire after 6 months. Your phone must be eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked (iPhone XS or newer and most recent Pixel / Galaxy flagships — check your exact model on the provider's compatibility list).

**Trade-off.** A foreign eSIM gives you data only — no +86 phone number. If a local app you need insists on one, get a physical SIM in addition.

### GFW Bypass and VPN Backup

- **Built-in bypass**: Airalo, Holafly and Nomad eSIM state that their data routes through gateways outside mainland China, so Google / WhatsApp / Instagram work on eSIM data with no separate VPN required (checked October 2026); Ubigi's statements are contradictory (see the note above). Some travelers report slower speeds in the evening and carry a backup VPN for that, or for Chinese hotel/airport Wi-Fi, which is still firewalled. Nomad cautions that a third-party VPN running on top of its eSIM "may interfere with its optimized functionality".
- **VPN data overhead** (only if you do run a VPN on top): a traveler guide estimates VPN encryption adds roughly 20–30% extra data usage. Factor this into any fixed-data plan.
- **Critical** (if relying on a VPN): Install and test your VPN app **before** arriving in China. VPN provider websites and app store entries are blocked inside the Great Firewall. A foreign travel eSIM's built-in bypass avoids this problem for eSIM data.

### Activation Timing and First-Hour Checklist

1. **1-2 days before departure**: Purchase eSIM and scan QR code to install the profile. Do NOT toggle it on yet.
2. **On landing**: Enable the eSIM data connection (and data roaming for that line). The validity clock starts on first connection.
3. **At the airport (while WiFi is available as fallback)**:
   - Test WhatsApp / Google / Instagram **on eSIM data** to confirm the built-in GFW bypass works.
   - If you carry a backup VPN, turn it on and confirm it connects.
   - If issues arise, troubleshoot while airport WiFi is still accessible.

### Data Usage Tips for Fixed-Data Plans

- **Download offline maps** for your planned cities before departure — saves significant data.
- Navigation, translation apps and a VPN are the heaviest data consumers. One traveler burned through 5 GB in 10 days with heavy Google Maps use.
- Budget example: an Airalo 5 GB plan (~$15) + pre-downloaded offline maps covers two weeks of light-to-moderate use, with no separate VPN needed.
- Consider an unlimited plan if you plan to upload photos, stream video, or use navigation continuously.

### Dual-SIM Strategy (for Travelers Who Need a +86 Number)

For travelers who need both unrestricted internet AND a +86 phone number:

1. **Slot 1 (eSIM)**: a foreign travel eSIM (e.g. Holafly or Airalo) for data + GFW bypass.
2. **Slot 2 (physical SIM)**: a local SIM for the +86 number — for SMS codes from local lifestyle apps where foreign numbers work inconsistently. It is **not** needed to register Alipay: Alipay accepts foreign mobile numbers, and Ant Group says visitors who bind an international card can use Alipay "without needing a mainland China bank account or phone number" (Ant International, 2024). Tourist SIM packages at airport counters and online run about US$12–17 for 7–10 days (see the plan table below).

This gives "best of both worlds" — full Western app access plus a local number for the apps that want one.

## How do I get a local SIM card in China?

### The Three State Carriers

China's mobile market is split among three state-owned operators. For a short-term tourist, none is dramatically better than the others — pick by store proximity unless you have a specific need.

| Carrier | Strengths | Customer hotline |
|---|---|---|
| China Mobile (CMCC) | Widest rural / inland coverage | 10086 |
| China Unicom | Usually the easiest choice for foreign visitors; stable in cities; Airalo's eSIM network | 10010 |
| China Telecom | Often good value; practical in southern China | 10000 |

(Hotline numbers per the Beijing and Shanghai municipal government guides; in Shanghai they are listed with the 021- prefix. Carrier strengths per chinaguidelines.com, checked 2026-10-08. Neither government guide states hours or an English-language menu option.)

### Free Tourist SIM Promotion (China Mobile)

According to traveler reports (2026, via this page's sources), China Mobile offers free SIM cards (4 cards with 10 GB each, totaling 40 GB) to foreign tourists at large flagship stores in tourist areas — passport required for registration. These SIMs operate behind the Great Firewall (no VPN included) but provide a +86 number. **This promotion is user-reported and has not been independently verified against official China Mobile channels (not re-checked in October 2026)** — treat it as unconfirmed, ask at a flagship store, and expect availability to vary by city and store.

### Where to Buy

| Channel | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Airport carrier counter** (Beijing Capital, Shanghai Pudong, Guangzhou Baiyun, etc.) | Usually higher than city stores | Convenient on arrival; plans are tourist-oriented |
| **Online, with airport or store pickup** (e.g. Trip.com, Nihao Mobile) | Similar to airport packages | Lets you compare plans before you fly; passport still shown in person at pickup |
| **Carrier flagship store in the city** | Lowest | More plan choices; better for longer stays — visit a large official carrier store rather than a small phone shop |

Both chinaguidelines.com and Trip.com describe airport plans as pricier than city-store or online options (checked 2026-10-08).

### Required Documents

- **Your original passport** — every source checked requires it. Trip.com: "All purchases of Chinese SIM cards require a valid passport … this is Chinese law, no exceptions."
- **Your visa — per Trip.com, for a carrier-store SIM.** Trip.com's comparison of SIM types says a local physical SIM "needs passport + visa to register; only at carrier stores", while its tips for airport and online tourist SIMs mention only the passport. The Shanghai government guide and chinaguidelines.com list only the passport. Bring both your passport and visa (or entry stamp) to be safe.
- Some stores may also ask for a local address (your hotel works); none of the sources checked lists this as a requirement.

### Real-Name Registration

Every Chinese SIM must be registered to the buyer's real identity. At the counter you will:

1. Have your passport scanned.
2. Have a live face photo taken and matched against your ID — required of carriers for new numbers since 1 December 2019 under an MIIT rule (TechNode, 2019-12-02).
3. Sign the customer agreement (usually in Chinese).

Registration is done in person. You can also order online: Shanghai's municipal guide notes that "online processing of a SIM card from the three major mobile companies can be done through Nihao Mobile," which offers English service, online ordering and delivery, and Trip.com sells tourist SIMs online for airport pickup. Either way, chinaguidelines.com notes "you still need to show your passport in person for real-name registration and SIM pickup".

- Check your balance by SMS: send "YE" to 10086 (China Mobile), "102" to 10010 (China Unicom) or "102" to 10001 (China Telecom) — per the Beijing municipal guide

## Why isn't my eSIM or SIM working in China?

| Symptom | Possible cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Registration rejected at counter | Passport or entry-status problem | Ask the store what is missing; try a large official carrier store |
| SMS verification codes don't arrive | Carrier's anti-spam filter on overseas-relayed SMS, or roaming gateway issue | Receive on a different carrier briefly, or use a Chinese friend's number |
| Data works but Google / YouTube don't | Expected — Chinese SIM goes through the Great Firewall | Use a VPN, or run a foreign eSIM in parallel |
| eSIM installed but no data in China | Data roaming is off for the eSIM line, or the eSIM isn't selected for mobile data | Select the eSIM for data and turn on data roaming for it; contact provider support if it still fails |
| Number stops working after months unused | Long-dormant prepaid numbers are commonly reported to be reclaimed | Keep the balance topped up; otherwise expect a fresh number next visit |

## Do I need a different SIM for Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan?

Mainland Chinese SIMs **do not work** in Hong Kong, Macau, or Taiwan without a separate roaming add-on — these are different jurisdictions despite the "China" branding. Holafly says its China eSIM is for mainland China only. Travelers crossing the border should plan for separate connectivity (a dedicated HK SIM, a roaming pack, or a regional Asia eSIM such as Airalo's or Ubigi's).

## Will my SIM or eSIM bypass the Great Firewall?

A Chinese SIM gives you Chinese-network internet, which blocks most non-Chinese services:

- Google (Search, Gmail, YouTube, Maps, Drive)
- Meta (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads)
- X / Twitter, the global version of TikTok, Reddit
- Most non-Chinese news sites

To access these on a Chinese SIM you need a VPN. Practically: install one **before** you arrive, since most commercial VPN provider sites and app store entries are themselves blocked from inside China. A foreign eSIM from a provider that routes data abroad sidesteps this.

See also: [[wiki/concepts/internet-connectivity.md]] for a full breakdown of the Great Firewall, VPN options, and apps that work without a VPN.
