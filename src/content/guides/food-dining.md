# Food, dining and delivery in China

## Which food precautions does CDC recommend for China?

CDC advises avoiding unpasteurized milk and undercooked meat and taking food, water and hand-hygiene precautions. If you have a food allergy or follow a vegetarian diet, write down ingredients to check with the restaurant before ordering.

[www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/asia/china.html)

## How can I order a meal in Shanghai?

Shanghai’s guide describes ordering on Meituan (美团) and Taobao Instant Commerce (淘宝闪购). Choose a delivery address, review the order and check the total before paying. Ask your accommodation where deliveries can be collected.

## What makes some Sichuan dishes taste numbing as well as spicy?

Sichuan pepper (花椒, huājiāo) gives some dishes their numbing sensation. This combination of numbing and spicy is called 麻辣 (málà) and is characteristic of Sichuan cuisine from Sichuan and Chongqing.

## Travel tip — Ordering without a shared language

Ask for a picture menu (图片菜单) or show a dish photo. For hotpot, ask whether a split pot (鸳鸯锅) is available if your group prefers different spice levels. For dietary restrictions, bring a written translation and confirm ingredients with staff. If the answer is unclear, choose another dish.

## Travel tip — When a digital menu is difficult to use

Ask whether there is a printed or picture menu (图片菜单). Try “我要这个” (I would like this one) while pointing to a dish. For small type, take a well-lit photo without glare and translate the still image. Ask for the bill and check the items before paying.

## Travel tip — Using photos to order at the table

You can use photos from a restaurant’s own delivery listing as a visual menu, even when eating in. Show the dish to staff and ask whether it is available at this branch today. If the menu is handwritten, ask someone to write the dish name so you can translate it.

## Travel tip — Ask about lard, oyster sauce and fish sauce

When ordering vegetarian food, you can show the server this question: 我吃素。请问这道菜里有猪油、蚝油或鱼露吗？ (I’m vegetarian. Does this dish contain lard, oyster sauce or fish sauce?) Name any other ingredients you avoid and ask whether the kitchen can leave them out.

## Travel tip — Breakfast and snack names to look for in Beijing, Shanghai and Xiamen

In Beijing, try asking a breakfast vendor for jianbing (煎饼), a savoury crêpe. One name you may see is 煎饼果子 (jianbing guozi). In Shanghai, look for 生煎包 (pan-fried buns), 蟹壳黄 (a flaky pastry named for its crab-shell shape) or 桂花糕 (sweet osmanthus cake). A Shanghai menu may call the buns 生煎馒头 (shengjian mantou); a traditional version has a pork filling and a crisp base. The pastries come in sweet and savoury varieties. Ask about the filling at the shop. In Xiamen, 花生汤 (peanut soup) is another option alongside 沙茶面 (shacha noodles) and 海蛎煎 (oyster omelette). Save the Chinese name and show it when ordering.

## Travel tip — A food card to show when ordering

Save the specific words you need: 我对花生过敏 (I am allergic to peanuts), 请不要放花生或花生油 (please leave out peanuts and peanut oil), 我对贝类过敏 (I am allergic to shellfish), 我对坚果过敏 (I am allergic to nuts), 我不能吃奶制品 (I cannot have dairy), 我不能吃鸡蛋 (I cannot have eggs), or 我不能吃小麦 (I cannot have wheat). For other preferences: 我是纯素食者 (I am vegan), 不要猪肉 (no pork), 不要味精 (no MSG), 这个菜有肉汤或猪油吗？ (does this dish contain meat stock or lard?). Describe your particular restriction; wheat and gluten are different terms. If cross-contact matters, ask about shared oil and utensils. A translated card cannot establish how a dish was prepared.

## Travel tip — Menu and drinking-water search words

Try showing these words at a counter: 开水 (boiled water), 矿泉水 (mineral water), 粤菜 (Cantonese cuisine), 沪菜 (Shanghai cuisine), 炸酱面 (noodles with soybean paste), 烤羊肉串 (grilled lamb skewers), 素食餐厅 (vegetarian restaurant), 鸡肉 (chicken) or 牛肉 (beef). If you want delivery, 美团外卖 (Meituan food delivery) is a useful search term.
