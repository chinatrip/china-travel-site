## Travel tip — Keeping a short offline phrase card

Save your hotel name and address, an emergency contact and any dietary needs in Chinese and your own language. Try “买单，谢谢” (the bill, please) and “可以写下来吗？” (could you write it down?). For a serious allergy or medical condition have a competent speaker check the translation in advance.

## Travel tip — Keeping conversations simple

Write down prices or times if you are unsure you heard them correctly. A small notepad can help when your phone battery runs out. Keep a hotel business card handy to show a driver.

## Travel tip — Asking for help in person

A small notebook can help you exchange numbers, draw a route or write a name. Ask someone at a staffed desk whether they can help or suggest an interpretation service.

## Travel tip — Getting important wording checked

If a booking or address translation looks odd, ask a staffed hotel or station desk to check the Chinese place name. For an allergy, bring a checked translation and show it to the restaurant; for a medical decision, ask the clinic for professional interpretation.

## Travel tip — How to say thank you to a driver (谢谢师傅) and check in at a hotel

Show your driver 这是我的目的地 (This is my destination) with the Chinese address. When you arrive, you can say 谢谢师傅 (Thank you, driver; xièxie shīfu). At reception, 你好，我预订了房间 (Hello, I have a reservation) pairs well with a screenshot of your booking name and dates. At a restaurant, 两位 (a table for two) or 不要香菜 (no coriander, please) can be easier to show than to pronounce.

## Travel tip — A few offline phrases for a ride or a station

Save a screenshot of your destination and these phrases: 请去这里 (please go here), 请在这里停 (please stop here), 我不会说中文 (I do not speak Chinese), 请问站台在哪里？ (where is the platform?), and 需要注册吗？ (do I need to register?). Show the address together with the words if you and the driver cannot hear each other.
